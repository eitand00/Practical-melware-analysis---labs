### Question 4
<img width="1052" height="80" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d18d8f1f-8ce2-4c0b-a3ea-3dd6c7316075" />

**Answer**  

The first step is to see the imports so we open dependency walker and look at the imports section.  
* <img width="1024" height="768" alt="1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0c431345-c3b8-4028-8057-89dba32f08f2" />
Next step is to see if we recognize any DLL file (if we don't recognize some of the DLL files we can check in the book for help).      
In our case the DLLs are "ADVAPI32.DLL" and "KERNEL32.DLL" and they access registry and services and controls memory files and hardware.
* From "ADVAPI32.DLL" we see the imports "AdjustTokenPrivileges", "LookupPrivilegeValueA", "OpenProcessToken" -  their job is to give the malware more privileges 
* <img width="1152" height="864" alt="1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5e57b0fb-3544-473b-9a71-d92fb2ff36eb" />

* From "KERNEL32.DLL" we see the imports "FindResourceA" , "LoadResource" , "SizeofResource" - the malware plant itself in legitimate EXE files
* From "KERNEL32.DLL" we see the imports "GetWindowsDirectoryA" , "CreateFileA" , "WriteFile" - the malware want to plant itself in a resourceful folder so it is saves itself in WindowsDirectory.
* From "KERNEL32.DLL" we see the imports "OpenProcess" , "CreateRemoteThread" , "WinExec"
* **WinExec** -  lunches an execulable.
* **OpenProcess , CreateRemoteThread** - inject the malware it extracted into a genuine  executable and lunch it so it seems like a legitimate process but it is doing the malware code instead of the original code.

* <img width="1152" height="864" alt="1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/57fb998a-cd78-4298-b936-3dc832baa13d" />
